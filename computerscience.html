Course Overview

This course is designed as a work-at-your-own-pace environment. This means you have the flexibility to move through the modules as quickly as you are able, allowing you to manage your own time and learning style.

⏰ Daily Attendance

While the coursework is flexible, our daily routine is not. Clock-ins are required every day before the first 5 minutes of class have passed.

🚀 Testing Out — One Unit at a Time

You can test out of the work — but you earn it one unit at a time, not all at once. Each of the seven units ends with a unit test, and that test is your chance to skip that unit's assignments.

What Your Unit Test Score Does
  • 80% or better: You're excused from that unit's assignments. You proved you already know it, so you don't have to prove it again.
  • 75% - 79%: You get one retest. There's a reteach day built into the calendar between the two, where we work on exactly the skills you missed. Score 80% on the retest and you're excused from that unit's work.
  • Below 75%, or a retest that doesn't reach 80%: You complete all of that unit's assignments. Not as a punishment — because the score says you don't have it yet, and the assignments are how you get it.

To test out of the whole class, you have to test out of every unit. There's no single exam that lets you skip the semester. Seven units, seven chances, and each one has to be earned on its own. A student who tests out of Unit 1 still takes the Unit 2 test. Everyone sits every unit test, every time — testing out of one unit never excuses you from the next one.

The Strict Rules

Be Here: The test must be taken in my classroom.

Use your Chromebook: It must be taken on a school Chromebook. No other device is permitted.

⚠️ Warning: If a student breaks either rule — especially by attempting a test outside my classroom — the attempt does not count, and that student completes all of the coursework for that unit.

🎓 The Semester Final — Everyone Takes It

Testing out of units does not excuse anyone from the semester final. Every student in this class takes it — a student who tested out of all seven units still sits the final.

What testing out earns you is the chance to take that final early. Once you've taken all seven unit tests, you may attempt the final whenever you're ready rather than waiting for the scheduled day.

The Outcomes
  • 80% or better: That's your final score. You're finished.
  • Below 80%: You get one retest — but you have to earn it first.

Before a second attempt is given, you have to show that you actually studied the material you missed. That's not a formality — there are two ways to do it:

  • Complete work in the unit or units the test showed you're weak in — the assignments exist precisely to teach the thing you missed.
  • Show your interactive notebook with additional notes and work on those topics.

No evidence of studying, no retest. "I'll do better this time" isn't studying, and taking the same test twice without learning anything in between doesn't help anyone.

Course Objectives

1 Foundations

Understanding the history and evolution of computing devices and data representation.

2 Computational Logic

Explaining key concepts including algorithms, abstraction, and data representation.

3 Programming Proficiency

Demonstrating skills with programming languages and software development tools.

Researching the relationship between computer science, society, and ethical responsibilities.

Understanding principles of digital safety and network defense.
