6.1 How Data Gets Organised

A pile of names, dates, and numbers dumped into one long list is technically "stored." It's just not answerable. Structure is what turns raw data into something you can actually query.

6.1.1 Four Ways to Structure Data

Flat File

Everything in one long, unstructured list. Fast to write, painful to search.

Table (Fields & Records)

Each row is one record, each column a consistent field. This is what a spreadsheet does for you automatically.

Hierarchical

Nested parent-child relationships — like folders inside folders.

Key-Value

A simple lookup: one unique key points to one value, built for speed.

Worked Example: Restructuring a Messy List

A flat list of "Jordan, 10th grade, A-, Chemistry" repeated 500 times can't answer "who's failing Chemistry?" without reading every single line. Turn it into a table with real fields — Student, Grade Level, Class, Score — and that same question becomes one sort and one glance.

The trap: students often think adding structure is pure upside. It isn't free — restructuring costs real effort up front. What it buys you is every query afterward becoming faster and actually possible. That's the tradeoff you're always making.

6.2 Where Data Lives

The same file stored on your laptop, on a network drive, and in the cloud survives completely different disasters. Location isn't just a convenience setting — it determines who can reach your data, how fast, and what happens when something fails.

6.2.1 Comparing Storage Locations

The trap: students often assume the most convenient storage option is automatically the safest. It's usually the opposite. Privacy and control tend to move inversely to convenience — cloud storage is effortless precisely because you've handed control of the keys to someone else. That's a decision, not an accident, and it's worth making on purpose.

Quick Check

Which storage option trades privacy for convenience, and why? (Answer: cloud storage — a third party holds both your data and the encryption keys, in exchange for access from anywhere.)
