Unit 6: Intermediate A&P — Project

The App Blueprint & Build Plan

Built in Google Sheets + Slides. 100 points.

Standards Addressed

Nevada Academic Content Standards for Computer Science & Integrated Technology: 9-12.AP.V.1 (linked lists/arrays vs. simple variables), 9-12.AP.M.1–2 (decompose into modules/procedures), 9-12.AP.PD.1 (design programs incorporating user feedback), 9-12.AP.PD.4 (team roles & collaborative tools), 9-12.AP.PD.5 (document design decisions) — the same standards Chapter 14 (The Array Refactor, The Structure Comparison), Chapter 15 (The Procedure Library, The Integrated Tool) and Chapter 16 (The Feedback Cycle, The License Audit, The Accessibility Pass) build toward.

Objective

Design a hypothetical app or program end-to-end: model its data, break it into real modules, and document a full software-development-lifecycle pass on it — including real feedback you actually got and acted on.

Deliverable: One Google Sheets tab (data structure comparison) + a Google Slides deck (everything else), minimum 6 slides. Turn in a PDF export of both, or a text file with your share links, below.

New to Google Slides or Sheets? Start here: Ch 4.0, The Office Suite, From Zero » · Spreadsheet Basics, From Zero »

Steps

Each step below links to the chapter section that actually teaches how to do it — do that first if you haven't already.

  1. Pick a hypothetical app or program idea (a to-do list, a grade tracker, a game, etc.).
  2. Sheets: Data Structure Comparison — model a real chunk of your app's data both as a list/array-style table and as separate simple variables, then write out why the list/array version scales better as the data grows. How to: Ch 14.1, Arrays »
  3. Slides: Module Breakdown — decompose your app into at least 3 real modules/procedures (e.g. "input handling," "score calculation," "display"), each with a one-sentence job description. How to: Ch 15.1, Procedures, Parameters & Return Values »
  4. Slides: SDLC Walkthrough — document each phase: Planning, Design, a real Feedback round (get actual feedback from a classmate and show what you changed because of it), Testing, and one specific Accessibility finding + fix. How to: Ch 16.1, The Lifecycle & Real User Feedback » · Ch 16.3, Accessibility »
  5. Slides: License Check — list every outside resource, image, icon, or font you used and its license/attribution. How to: Ch 16.2, Licensing & Intellectual Property »
