Unit 1: Digital Citizenship — Project

The Digital Citizenship Case File

Built in Google Slides. 100 points.

Standards Addressed

Nevada Academic Content Standards for Computer Science & Integrated Technology: 9-12.DC.B.1, 9-12.DC.B.2 (digital footprint & identity), 9-12.DC.A.1 (safe/legal/ethical use), 9-12.DC.D.1 (responsible content & attribution) — the same standards Chapters 1–2's labs (The Footprint Audit, The Conduct Case Studies, The Threat Recognition Lab, The Data Trail, The Attribution Lab, The Privacy Position) build toward.

Objective

Build a case file that proves you can actually apply digital citizenship — not just define it. You'll audit your own digital footprint, investigate a real documented case where digital citizenship failed, and write your own code of conduct based on what you find.

Deliverable: A Google Slides deck, minimum 8 slides. Share your deck with view access and turn in a PDF export (File → Download → PDF Document) below, or a text file with your share link.

Steps
  1. Title Slide — your name and the case file title.
  2. Footprint Audit — search your own name/handles (or an invented persona, if you'd rather not use your real one). List or screenshot what's publicly visible, sorted into Safe, Risky, and Should Be Deleted.
  3. Real-World Case Study — pick a real, documented digital-citizenship failure: a data breach, a post that ended someone's career, a cyberbullying case that made news, a deepfake/misinformation incident. Summarize what actually happened.
  4. Threat & Risk ID — name the specific technical/security threat involved (phishing, a weak password, oversharing, malware, etc.).
  5. Ethics & Privacy Analysis — whose privacy or rights were violated, and exactly how.
  6. Sourcing & Attribution — a Works Cited slide. Every fact and image used anywhere in the deck needs a real citation.
  7. Your Code of Conduct — 5 specific, personal rules you're committing to, each one tied back to something you learned from the case study.
  8. Reflection — what would you have done differently if you were the person in the case study?
