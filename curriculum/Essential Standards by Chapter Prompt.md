# Prompt: Restructure Essential Standards by Chapter, Not Unit

Paste this into your counterpart, with the Essential Learning Year-at-a-Glance document attached/pasted alongside it.

---

> You are restructuring a curriculum planning document for a Nevada high school Web Design and Development
> program (Year 1 and Year 2). The document is currently organized with **Unit as the primary heading** —
> each Unit groups together 1-3 chapters, and each chapter's standards are listed in a row nested inside
> that Unit. For example, today it looks like this:
>
> > **Unit 1: The Developer's World**
> > - Essential Learning — Ch 1: The Developer's World. Soft Skills, CTSOs, and Internet vs. Web Structure.
> >   Standards: NV 1.1.1, NV 2.1.2, WRS 1.1.4, 1.1.2. (CFA: Sept 11) — 7 Blocks
> > - Essential Learning — Ch 2: The "Rules". Web Law, Ethics & Accessibility.
> >   Standards: NV 3.2.1, WRS 1.1.5. (CFA: Sept 18) — 4 Blocks
>
> Your job: **flip the hierarchy so Chapter is the primary heading, not Unit.** Every chapter becomes its
> own top-level entry with its own standards listed directly under it. The Unit name becomes a small
> secondary label attached to each chapter (e.g. a subtitle or tag), not a container multiple chapters sit
> inside. Using the same example, the restructured result should look like this:
>
> > **Chapter 1: The Developer's World** *(Unit 1: The Developer's World)*
> > Soft Skills, CTSOs, and Internet vs. Web Structure.
> > Standards: NV 1.1.1, NV 2.1.2, WRS 1.1.4, 1.1.2. (CFA: Sept 11) — 7 Blocks
> >
> > **Chapter 2: The "Rules"** *(Unit 1: The Developer's World)*
> > Web Law, Ethics & Accessibility.
> > Standards: NV 3.2.1, WRS 1.1.5. (CFA: Sept 18) — 4 Blocks
>
> ### Rules
>
> 1. **Do not drop or alter any data.** Every standard code, learning target, CFA date, and block count must
>    survive the restructuring exactly as written. This is a reorganization, not a rewrite.
> 2. **One chapter = one entry**, every time, with no chapter's standards split across multiple entries and
>    no two chapters merged into one entry.
> 3. **Keep the Unit name as a reference tag on each chapter** (e.g. in parentheses, or a small subtitle) so
>    the reader can still see which unit a chapter belongs to — just don't use Unit as the grouping structure
>    anymore.
> 4. **Apply this to the entire document** — both Year 1 and Year 2 (all 16 chapters), including any
>    standalone Project/Milestone/Capstone rows that currently sit between chapters under the same Unit;
>    keep those as their own entries too, tagged with the Unit they belong to, rather than folding them into
>    a neighboring chapter.
> 5. **Preserve chapter order** (Chapter 1 through Chapter 16) as the document's top-to-bottom order — do
>    not re-sort by anything else.
> 6. If any standard is currently listed only at the Unit level with no specific chapter attached, flag it
>    explicitly rather than guessing which chapter it belongs to.
