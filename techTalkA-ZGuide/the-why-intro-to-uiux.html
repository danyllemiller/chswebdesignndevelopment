Chapter 4: Tech Talk: Your A-Z Glossary

Master the language of UI/UX with these core terms.

Essential Terminology

Accessibility (a11y)
Designing so people with disabilities can use your site.
Balance (Principle of Design)
Visual "weight" distributed evenly or intentionally unevenly. Can be symmetrical (mirrored) or asymmetrical (offset but still balanced).
Branding Message
A short, specific statement naming who a business serves, what outcome it delivers, and what makes it different — not a generic slogan.
Breadcrumbs
A secondary navigation scheme that reveals the user's location.
Card
A UI pattern that groups related info (image, headline, button) into a container.
Cognitive Load
The amount of mental effort required to use an interface. Keep it low!
Color Harmony (Complementary, Analogous, Triadic, Monochromatic)
The mathematical relationships between colors on the color wheel that determine which combinations work well together.
Contrast
The difference in brightness between text and background. High contrast = readable.
Elements of Design
The 7 raw visual building blocks of any design: Line, Shape, Color, Value, Texture, Space, and Form.
Emphasis (Principle of Design)
Making the single most important element the visual focal point of a layout, usually through size, color, or position.
Figma
A web-based vector graphics editor and prototyping tool.
Hamburger Menu
A toggle menu for hiding navigation on mobile devices.
Hero Section
The introductory section of a website, usually containing a key image and message.
High-Fidelity
A design that looks exactly like the final product.
Jakob's Law
Users prefer your site to work the same way as all the other sites they already know.
Leading (Line-Height)
The vertical space between lines of text. Best practice for body copy: 1.5 to 1.7. Too tight and lines feel cramped; the eye loses its place.
Line (Element of Design)
A mark connecting two points. Divides sections, underlines links, and guides the eye across a layout.
Mobile-First
A design philosophy that starts with the mobile viewport and scales up.
Mockup
A static design representation used to demonstrate the look of the site.
Modal
A pop-up window that appears on top of the page, blocking other interactions until it is closed.
Movement (Principle of Design)
Guiding the viewer's eye through a layout in a deliberate path, using lines, arrows, or a sequence of elements.
Principles of Design
The 8 rules for combining the Elements of Design into an intentional layout: Balance, Contrast, Emphasis, Movement, Pattern, Proportion, Rhythm, Unity.
Proportion (Principle of Design)
The size relationship between elements — a headline should be dramatically larger than body text, or the hierarchy collapses.
Rhythm (Principle of Design)
A repeated visual "beat," like evenly spaced cards in a grid, that the eye can comfortably scan across.
Shape (Element of Design)
A closed 2D area, like a button or card. Rounded shapes read as friendly; sharp corners read as serious and technical.
Space / White Space (Element of Design)
The empty area around and between elements. An active design tool, not wasted room — it creates breathing room and groups related items.
Texture (Element of Design)
The surface quality something appears to have on screen — a drop shadow makes a card feel like it's floating above the page.
Tracking (Letter-Spacing)
The uniform spacing between all characters in a block of text. Wide tracking on all-caps headings reads as a luxury technique; never use it on body text.
UI (User Interface)
The graphical layout of an application.
UI Pattern
A reusable solution to a common design problem (e.g., Cards, Modals, Breadcrumbs).
Usability Testing
The process of observing real users trying to complete tasks on your site to find design flaws.
Unity (Principle of Design)
The feeling that every element belongs to the same design, achieved through consistent color, spacing, and typography.
UX (User Experience)
How a user interacts with and experiences a product.
Value (Element of Design)
How light or dark something is, independent of color/hue. Value — not color — is what actually creates contrast and readability.
Wireframe
A basic, skeletal visual guide used in interface design.
60-30-10 Rule
A color-application ratio: 60% Primary color, 30% Secondary color, 10% Accent color reserved for calls to action.
