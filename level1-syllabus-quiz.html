Syllabus Quiz

Web Design I | Instructor: Ms. Miller

12 questions on the grading policy, Career Readiness, tardies, and classroom expectations. Answer every question, then check your score.

1. In Web Design I, what percentage of your grade comes from Assignments & Skills?

Assignments & Skills are 50% of your grade, the biggest category. The quality of your daily work matters most.

2. How is the rest of your Web Design I grade split up?

Assignments & Skills (50%) + Projects, Tests & Quizzes (20%) + Semester Exams (20%) + Career Readiness (10%) = 100%.

3. How do you earn the $5 bonus on your bi-weekly simulated paycheck?

Every on-time clock-in through the Employee Portal earns a $5 bonus on your bi-weekly simulated paycheck. Tardiness is tracked, just like at a real job.

4. Your Guild Score is a separate class ranking. What three things build it, and how much does each count?

Guild Score = Grades 50% + Habits (My Planner) 25% + Attendance (Clock-In) 25%. Your habit data is averaged over the last 28 days.

5. You walk in after the bell. What do you need to do so you aren't marked absent?

Everyone is marked absent at the start of class. Come in quietly, fill out a Tardy Form, and turn it in before you leave. If the form isn't turned in, the absence stays on your record.

6. At which tardy does your teacher first contact home about it?

The first call home comes at the third tardy. Tardies reset each grading period, and every 10 on-time arrivals in a row removes one tardy from your count.

7. You want to redo a project to improve your grade. What's the rule?

Redos are welcome: redo any project until you're satisfied, within the current or the very next unit. There is no extra credit.

8. You know you'll need extra time on an assignment. What's the correct move?

Life happens. Ask for an extension by email at least 24 hours ahead, not the day it's due. Late work without an extension can earn at most 60% (D-).

9. A classmate emails you their finished HTML file and you turn it in as your own. What happens?

That's plagiarism, and both the giver and the taker get a zero. Helping a classmate find a bug is encouraged. Handing over your finished file is not.

10. What's true about using AI tools like ChatGPT, Claude, or Copilot on your work?

AI can help you learn and brainstorm. Turning in AI's work as your own, without understanding it or saying you used it, is not allowed. If you use it, cite it and be able to explain every line.

11. You're stuck on a coding bug. What does the "Three Then Me" rule say to do?

Check three resources (Google, your digital notebook, or a classmate) before asking the teacher. Real developers solve most problems this way.

12. What food and drink is allowed at the computers?

No food at the computers. Only water in a sealable, spill-proof bottle, kept on the floor or in your backpack. Damaging equipment means paying to repair or replace it.
