Classroom Standards & Accountability | Instructor: Ms. Miller

Progressive Discipline

Students must show respect for themselves and others at all times. Any derogatory language or disrespect to peers, instructors, or school staff is unacceptable.

1 Teacher Warning

A brief private meeting outside the classroom to address the issue.

2 Resolution Discussion

Collaborative student/teacher plan of action to resolve the conflict.

3 Parent Contact & Detention

Phone call home and assigned lunchtime detention.

4 Referral Stage

Official referral to Dean or Counselor based on the circumstance.

5 Formal Conference

Required Parent-Teacher-Dean conference to address persistent issues.

*Documentation is maintained for all disciplinary conversations.

Tardies Policy

The first few minutes of class carry the warm-up, the day's directions, and often the piece of instruction everything else builds on. A student who arrives five minutes late has usually missed the part that would have made the rest of the period make sense. Repeated over a semester, that adds up in a way that shows in their understanding well before it shows in their grade.

How a Late Arrival Gets Cleared

Every student is marked absent at the start of class as a routine step. When a student arrives late, they come in quietly, take a Tardy Form from the tray by the door, fill it out during the first break in instruction, and turn it in before they leave. At the end of the school day, attendance is corrected — changed from absent to tardy, or to present if the student had a signed pass from another staff member.

If the form isn't turned in, the absence stands. The form is what protects a student's record, and an absence is considerably harder to undo than a tardy.

As Tardies Accumulate

Tardies are counted per grading period and reset at the start of each new one. The steps are progressive, and the early ones are conversations rather than penalties.

1 First Tardy

Brief private check-in. No further consequence.

2 Second Tardy

Five-minute conference and a short written reflection identifying one specific change.

3 Third Tardy

Parent/guardian contacted by phone or email, and a short support plan is built together. Missed class time is made up during lunch or before/after school.

4 Fourth Tardy

Scheduled restorative session, a revised plan, and the student's counselor is looped in. Parent/guardian is notified of the outcome.

5 Fifth Tardy & Beyond

Administrative referral, with a meeting requested including parent/guardian, counselor, and an administrator.

Tardies with a signed pass from a staff member, the office, the nurse, or a counselor are excused and do not count. Students can also remove one tardy from their count for every ten consecutive on-time arrivals — the count can go down as well as up.

How This Affects Grades

Attendance is part of the grade — not as a point deduction for arriving late, but as one of the graded Career Readiness habits. Clock-In Punctuality feeds directly into the Guild Score: Career Readiness is 10% of the overall grade, and Attendance (Clock-In) makes up 25% of that Career Readiness category. Every on-time clock-in also earns a $5 bonus on a student's bi-weekly simulated paycheck. Showing up on time is treated the way it would be at a real job — it's tracked, it's rewarded, and it counts. See the Grading Policy for the full breakdown.

How Parents Can Help

The first call home comes at the third tardy, and it's meant to be a conversation rather than a complaint. Persistent lateness usually has a cause underneath it — a transportation problem, a schedule that doesn't allow enough passing time, a health issue, anxiety about the class, or something happening at home. Finding that out early, and working around it, beats escalating through steps that don't address the real problem.

Two things help most: talk with your student about the Tardy Form — it takes ninety seconds and it's the difference between a tardy and an absence on their record — and if you already know that getting to class on time is going to be difficult for your student, say so before the third tardy rather than after. There's usually something that can be built around it.

Computer Lab Rules: Food & Drink Policy

Our lab is full of shared, expensive equipment that has to keep working for the next class too. These rules exist to protect that equipment, not to be difficult about lunch.

Rules
  • No Food at Workstations: All food must be kept securely inside a backpack or eaten outside the classroom.
  • Approved Drinks Only: Only water in a sealable container with a spill-proof lid is permitted.
  • Floor Storage: Approved water bottles must be kept on the floor or in a backpack.
  • Clean Equipment: Hands must be entirely clean and free of grease, sugar, or crumbs before touching hardware.
Consequences
1 1st Offense

Verbal warning. Put away or dispose of the item.

2 2nd Offense

Relocation away from the computer, alternative bookwork, or loss of participation points.

3 3rd Offense

Parent/guardian contact and a formal disciplinary referral.

Severe Clause: Damage to equipment results in immediate financial responsibility for replacement or repair.
